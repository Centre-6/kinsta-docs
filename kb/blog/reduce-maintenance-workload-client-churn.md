Imagine that you are working on an important project for one of your biggest clients. Suddenly, your team must stop working on it and urgently deal with problems caused by hackers on a small client’s website.

Nobody likes working in emergency mode. However, without an adequate operational strategy, when a customer’s site is under attack or crashes due to a failed update, you must rush to take emergency action.

This approach may be a serious obstacle to your agency’s growth because it distracts your team from your core business. It reveals the lack of a clear growth strategy. It can cause loss of income and business opportunities, reduce productivity, and undermine the trust between your agency and your clients.

Switching from firefighting to future-proofing can help your business grow and make your workflow more efficient and reliable. This post aims to help you understand the differences between the reactive approach (fighting problems as they arise) and the proactive approach (getting ready for the future). You will learn how moving from the former to the latter approach can prepare your business for success.

## The proactive approach to future-proofing: what it is and what it does

A **future-proofing strategy** requires a careful analysis of risks and opportunities, as well as proactive planning of the necessary steps to maintain security. It also involves regular site maintenance and a set of tools and services that may help your agency plan and schedule activities before potential problems occur. In short, if you take action before things go wrong, you won’t have to deal with many issues.

![The firefighting approach](https://kinsta.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/the-firefighting-approach.png)

The firefighting approach

By adopting a future-proofing approach, organizations anticipate potential problems, mitigate risks, increase resilience, and keep up with the competition.

Like any organization, web agencies must spot and figure out the risks ahead of time. These risks also include:

- Security vulnerabilities and cyber attacks
- Compatibility issues that may arise after an update
- Inadequate management of credentials and access
- Lack of backups
- Performance issues

To prevent most problems from arising, agencies that take a proactive approach to WordPress site management automate repetitive tasks, schedule activities, implement best practices against common security risks, and choose a secure and reliable hosting infrastructure.

![The future-proofing approach](https://kinsta.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/the-future-proofing-approach.png)

## Automation and monitoring

Updating the core and extensions of your WordPress website, monitoring site uptime, performing regular malware scans, and backing up site files and databases require a considerable amount of work each month. Neglecting these tasks is not an option, as keeping a site secure and up to date is vital for an online business. However, manually **performing updates for each client site can be challenging and may mask****costs that grow over time.**

Luckily, some helpful third-party services and software tools can automate most repetitive tasks. Because there are many different tasks, you should sign up for many services and learn how to manage different dashboards. This may take some time, and there are usually fees associated with these services.

Yet many of these tasks could be handled by your hosting provider. Kinsta, for example, offers a wide range of automation features and built-in services, freeing agency owners from the responsibility and costs associated with automation and monitoring tasks.

Let’s take a closer look at these services and tools.

### 1. Automated and secure updates

If you’ve been using WordPress for a while, you’ll be familiar with the tedious and risky process of updating the core, plugins, and themes. Updates can fail due to fatal PHP errors or connection interruptions. Additionally, conflicts may arise between plugins or the theme after the update, causing the site to crash or malfunction.

![WordPress Core releases](https://kinsta.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/wordpress-core-releases.png)

WordPress Core releases (Source: [WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/news/category/releases/))

Therefore, updating any part of a WordPress site — the core, a plugin, or the active theme — must always be done with caution. After each update, it is also necessary to thoroughly check the site’s functionality.

The ideal update process consists of the following steps:

- Back up the site before updating it.
- Perform the updates in a staging environment.
- Check that the staging site works and displays correctly.
- If everything works as expected, push the staging site to production.
- Ensure the live site works correctly and the pages display as expected.

What should you do if something goes wrong during the process? You must restore the site from a working backup, identify the problem, and start again.

![WooCommerce Changelog page](https://kinsta.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/woocommerce-changelog.png)

WooCommerce Changelog page (Source [WooCommerce.com](https://developer.woocommerce.com/changelog/))

How long do you update your clients’ websites each month? This additional workload incurs an hourly cost, which is the average cost of working time in your industry.

Fortunately, WordPress can now [automatically update](https://kinsta.com/blog/wordpress-automatic-updates/) the core, plugins, and themes, and even [roll back](https://kinsta.com/blog/wordpress-automatic-updates/#rollback-for-automatic-updates) the core and plugins in case of failure.

However, even WordPress automatic updates do not eliminate all the potential risks of failure. For example, suppose a plugin update is performed correctly, but the new version is incompatible with other plugins or the current PHP version. In that case, WordPress may not be able to perform a rollback. This means that, even with WordPress automatic updates enabled, it is essential to create backups and regularly monitor the uptime. ### 2. Automatic backup and one-click restore

Your WordPress site is always at risk of security breaches and interruptions. That’s why **you must always have at least one recent clean backup of your live site.**

How frequently you perform backups depends on the type of website you manage. One weekly backup may be sufficient for a [showcase website](https://kinsta.com/blog/portfolio-website/) or personal blog with a few new posts published monthly, while news or big e-commerce websites that are updated several times a day may need hourly backups.

Manually performing a backup is not always easy and may require a specialised professional. For this reason, it is crucial to use plugins or tools that automatically back up the site at regular intervals — weekly, daily, or even several times a day, depending on your needs. These tools should also allow you to quickly restore the site from a backup with just a few clicks.

However, WordPress automatic backup plugins such as UpdraftPlus and Duplicator come with fees that can significantly increase the cost of maintenance.

## Strategic planning

Not everything can be automated when managing a WordPress site. On highly complex sites, especially those with custom code, incompatibilities may arise that must be handled manually. The same applies when you have implemented integrations with external services that may stop working following an update or a manual change to the code.

In these cases, having staging environments where you can safely test changes before implementing them is crucial.

Staging environments also enable agencies to provide proactive maintenance packages. Rather than charging client companies for each intervention, agencies can offer [comprehensive maintenance packages](https://kinsta.com/blog/website-as-a-service/) supported by high-level hosting. These packages can significantly increase revenues without creating unmanageable workloads.

### 1. Staging environments

[Staging environments](https://kinsta.com/blog/wordpress-staging-site/) come in handy in many situations. One common use is to run WordPress, plugins, and theme updates in a safe environment to ensure everything works correctly. Once you have updated and tested your website, you can push the staging to production.

Staging environments are essential for complex site changes, such as custom code that may not work as expected after a plugin update, or when upgrading to a new PHP version. In such instances, making changes directly in production is not a viable solution.

If your host does not provide staging environments, you can always use a plugin such as [WP Staging](https://it.wordpress.org/plugins/wp-staging/), which is ideal if your site runs “on a weak shared hosting server”.
