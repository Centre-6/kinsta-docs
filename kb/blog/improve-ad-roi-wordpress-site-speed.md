## How slow sites destroy ad performance

Slow page loads not only annoy users but also directly undermine [PPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay-per-click) and [SEO](https://kinsta.com/blog/headless-wordpress-seo/) ROI. Below are multiple examples of how this happens, accompanied by real numbers to illustrate the true cost of such lag.

### 1. Bounce rate spikes, traffic drains

Industry [studies](https://business.google.com/us/think/) have long shown that even a two-second delay can double bounce rates, and visitors are far [more likely to abandon](https://www.akamai.com/newsroom/press-release/akamai-releases-spring-2017-state-of-online-retail-performance-report) a page once load time stretches past three seconds.

And every time that happens, it’s wasted ad spend. You paid for the click but received no lead or conversion, resulting in another charge to the campaign budget.

### 2. Conversion rates tank with each added second

The longer a page takes to load, the fewer people stick around to act. Research [consistently shows](https://business.google.com/us/think/) that sites loading in one second [convert several times better](https://portent.com/blog/analytics/research-site-speed-hurting-everyones-revenue.htm) than those taking five seconds to load. For agencies, that gap is the difference between hitting ROI targets and explaining to clients why results fell flat.

### 3. Quality Score & CPC suffer

Google factors landing page experience into [Quality Score](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118?hl=en), and speed is a big part of that. Slow sites pull Quality Scores down, which pushes CPC up and hurts ad placement. In other words, the same budget buys fewer results.

Generally speaking, improving page speed leads directly to more “bang for buck” in ad campaigns, which translates into lower CPC and better conversion-to-cost.

### 4. SEO & organic traffic get knocked off track

[Core Web Vitals](https://kinsta.com/blog/core-web-vitals/)[,](https://kinsta.com/blog/core-web-vitals/) such as [LCP](https://kinsta.com/blog/largest-contentful-paint/), [INP](https://web.dev/articles/inp), and [CLS,](https://kinsta.com/blog/cumulative-layout-shift/) are established ranking signals. If a landing page loads slowly or responds sluggishly, rankings slip. That means you’re not only paying more per click on ads, but also losing free organic traffic you could have earned with a faster site.

### 5. Brand trust, retention, and long-term cost

People don’t come back to slow sites. [47% of people](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/performance/more/website-performance-conversion-rates) expect pages to load in under three seconds. When load time drifts higher, satisfaction drops, and they’re less likely to return.

Poor [performance](https://kinsta.com/blog/ecommerce-performance/) erodes trust, hurts retention, and reduces lifetime value. For agencies, that means wasted spend today and harder client conversations tomorrow.
